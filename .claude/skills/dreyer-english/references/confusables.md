# The Confusables

Word pairs (and small clusters) that spellcheck won't catch, from *Dreyer's English* ch. 10. Distinctions condensed; consult a dictionary for edge cases.

## A LOT/ALLOT, ALLOTTED, ALLOTTING
- A lot of something is a great deal of it.
- To allot is to assign.

## ADVANCE/ADVANCED
- "Advance" (verb: move forward; noun: forward movement or preliminary payment; adjective: beforehand, as in "in advance").
- "Advanced" means ahead of the norm in progress or complexity. Bound galleys are "advance" copies, not "advanced."

## ADVERSE/AVERSE
- "Adverse" means unfavorable or harmful (adverse weather).
- "Averse" means opposed to or repelled by (averse to olives).

## AFFECT/EFFECT
- Usual case: "affect" is the verb (to influence), "effect" is the noun (a result).
- But "affect" is also a noun (observable emotional expression), and "effect" is also a verb (to bring about, as in "effect change").

## AID/AIDE
- To aid is to help.
- An aide is an assistant.

## AISLE/ISLE
- Aisles are the passages between seats or shelves.
- Isles are islands (usually small).

## ALL RIGHT/ALRIGHT
- "All right" is standard. "Alright" is widely considered nonstandard; avoid it in formal writing.

## ALLUDE/ALLUSION/ALLUSIVE/ELUDE/ELUSIVE
- To allude is to refer to indirectly; such a reference is an allusion (adjective: allusive).
- To elude is to escape. Something hard to grasp or recall is elusive.

## ALTAR/ALTER
- An altar is a raised structure used in religious ceremonies.
- To alter is to change.

## ALTERNATE/ALTERNATIVE
- An alternate is a substitute or every-other (alternate Wednesdays, alternate layers); as an adjective it can mean "succeeding by turns."
- Alternatives are options to choose among. "Alternative" can also mean outside the norm (alternative medicine).
- One's other identity is an "alter ego."

## AMBIGUOUS/AMBIVALENT
- Ambiguous means unclear, open to more than one interpretation.
- Ambivalent means having mixed feelings. Meaning is ambiguous; attitude is ambivalent.

## AMOK/AMUCK
- To run amok is to go into a wild frenzy.
- "Amuck" is a variant spelling; "amok" is now standard.

## AMUSE/BEMUSE/BEMUSED
- To amuse is to entertain.
- To bemuse is to confuse or bewilder (not, properly, to mildly amuse).

## ANYMORE/ANY MORE
- "Anymore" means any longer or nowadays (not in Kansas anymore).
- "Any more" means an additional amount (no more pie).

## APPRAISE/APPRISE
- To appraise is to assess or evaluate.
- To apprise is to inform.

## ASSURE/ENSURE/INSURE
- To assure is to reassure a person (relieve doubt).
- To ensure is to make something certain.
- To insure relates to insurance (compensation for loss).

## BAITED/BATED
- A trap is baited (fitted with bait).
- "Bated" (nearly always in "bated breath") means held or suspended in tense anticipation.

## BAKLAVA/BALACLAVA
- Baklava is a nut-and-honey filo pastry.
- A balaclava is a head-covering hood (ski mask).

## BAWL/BALL
- To bawl one's eyes out is to weep hard.
- "Ball" is the wrong word for it.

## BERG/BURG
- A berg is an iceberg.
- A burg is a slangy, often dismissive term for a town.

## BESIDE/BESIDES
- "Beside" means next to.
- "Besides" means other than, or in addition to.

## BLACK OUT/BLACKOUT
- The verb is "black out" (lose consciousness).
- The noun is "blackout" (loss of consciousness, power failure, or suppression of information).

## BLOND/BLONDE
- "Blond" works as the general adjective and the noun for a fair-haired man.
- "Blonde" (feminine, from French) is the noun for a fair-haired woman; if used as an adjective, apply only to women. It carries cultural baggage, so use it carefully.

## BOARDER/BORDER
- A boarder rents a room.
- A border divides one place from another.

## BORN/BORNE
- Use "born" for birth, literal or metaphorical (born yesterday).
- Use "borne" for things carried or produced (insect-borne, has borne fruit).

## BREACH/BREECH/BROACH/BROOCH
- To breach is to break open or pierce; a breach is a rupture or violation (also a whale leaping).
- "Breech" refers to the rear (a breech birth is buttocks-first).
- To broach a subject is to raise it.
- A brooch is decorative jewelry.

## BREATH/BREATHE/BREADTH
- "Breath" is the noun; "breathe" is the verb. (Easy to write one for the other.)
- "Breadth" means width.

## BULLION/BOUILLON
- Bullion is metal (gold/silver).
- Bouillon is broth.

## CACHE/CACHET
- A cache is a hiding place or hidden collection (verb: to hide). Pronounced "cash."
- Cachet is prestige or distinction. Pronounced "ka-shay."

## CALLOUS/CALLUS
- To be callous is to be hard-hearted.
- A callus is a thickening of the skin.

## CANVAS/CANVASS
- Canvas is cloth (sails, painting surface).
- To canvass is to solicit votes or opinions.

## CAPITAL/CAPITOL
- A capital is a city, an uppercase letter, funds, or a serious (capital) crime.
- A capitol is the building housing a legislature.

## CARAT/KARAT/CARET/CARROT
- A carat is a unit of gemstone weight.
- A karat measures gold purity (24-karat is pure).
- A caret is the insertion symbol (^).
- A carrot is the vegetable.

## CASUAL/CAUSAL
- Casual means easygoing, informal.
- Causal means relating to cause and effect. (Nearly identical in print, opposite in meaning.)

## CHORD/CORD
- A chord is simultaneous musical notes (also "strike a chord" emotionally).
- A cord is a string of threads. It's "vocal cords," not "vocal chords."

## CITE/SIGHT/SITE
- To cite is to quote or attribute.
- A site is a location (physical or web).
- A sight is a thing seen (also the aiming device on a firearm).

## CLASSIC/CLASSICAL
- A classic is an excellent or defining example.
- "Classical" refers to ancient Greece/Rome or the orchestral music tradition.

## CLIMACTIC/CLIMATIC
- Climactic relates to a climax.
- Climatic relates to climate/weather.

## COME/CUM
- "Come" is the verb (to climax); "cum" is the noun for the fluid.
- As a conjunction, "cum" means combined with (desk-cum-bureau), hyphenated and set in roman.

## COMPLEMENT/COMPLEMENTARY/COMPLIMENT/COMPLIMENTARY
- To complement is to go well with; complementary things enhance each other.
- A compliment is praise; complimentary can also mean free of charge.

## CONFIDANT/CONFIDANTE
- "Confidant" works for anyone you confide in.
- "Confidante" is only a woman. (Same pattern as fiancé/fiancée.)

## CONSCIENCE/CONSCIOUS
- Your conscience is your inner sense of right and wrong.
- To be conscious is to be awake and aware.

## CONTINUAL/CONTINUOUS
- "Continual" means recurring with interruptions.
- "Continuous" means ceaseless, without a break.

## CORONET/CORNET
- A coronet is a small crown.
- A cornet is a trumpetlike instrument.

## CRITERION/CRITERIA
- "Criterion" is singular; "criteria" is plural.

## CROCHET/CROTCHETY/CROTCHET
- To crochet is to do needlework with a crochet hook.
- To be crotchety is to be grouchy.
- One's crotchets are eccentric notions or habits (Brit. also: a quarter note).

## CUE/QUEUE
- A cue is a signal (verb: to signal, or "cue up" = get ready to play).
- A queue is a line of people (verb: to line up). Also a braided ponytail.

## DAIRY/DIARY
- Meanings are clear; the risk is mistyping one for the other.

## DEFUSE/DIFFUSE
- To defuse is to remove danger (a bomb, a tense situation).
- "Diffuse" as an adjective means unconcentrated; as a verb, to spread out.

## DEMUR/DEMURE/DEMURRAL
- To demur is to object (often politely); the noun is demur or demurral (also meaning delay).
- To be demure is to be modest or reserved.

## DESCENDANT/DESCENDENT
- "Descendant" is the noun (offspring).
- "Descendent" is the adjective (moving downward). You almost always want the noun.

## DESERT/DESSERT
- A desert is the arid place; a dessert is the sweet course.
- Comeuppance is "just deserts" (what one deserves), not "desserts."

## DISASSOCIATE/DISSOCIATE
- Both mean to sever a connection. "Dissociate" is preferred by some; "disassociate" is not wrong.

## DISCREET/DISCRETE
- Discreet means circumspect, tactful.
- Discrete means separate and distinct.

## EEK/EKE
- "Eek!" is the cry of alarm.
- To eke (out) is to obtain with difficulty and barely.

## EMIGRATE/IMMIGRATE
- One emigrates from a place and immigrates to a place. (Used of moving between nations/continents.)

## EMINENT/IMMINENT/IMMANENT
- Eminent means renowned.
- Imminent means about to happen.
- Immanent means inherent, built in.

## ENVELOP/ENVELOPE
- "Envelop" is the verb (to surround).
- "Envelope" is the noun (for a letter).

## EPIGRAM/EPIGRAPH
- An epigram is a witty, pithy saying.
- An epigraph is a quotation set at the start of a book or chapter.

## EVERYDAY/EVERY DAY
- "Everyday" is an adjective (an everyday occurrence).
- "Every day" is an adverb (I go to work every day).

## EVOKE/INVOKE
- To evoke is to call to mind (figurative).
- To invoke is to summon or call upon in actual practice. Confine evoking to the figurative, invoking to the actual.

## EXERCISE/EXORCISE
- Exercise is what you do at the gym.
- Exorcise is what you do to demons. Someone agitated is "exercised," not "exorcised."

## FARTHER/FURTHER
- "Farther" for literal physical distance.
- "Further" for figurative degree or time. When in doubt, use "further."

## FAUN/FAWN
- A faun is a mythical man-goat creature.
- A fawn is a young deer (also a pale brown color); to fawn is to grovel for favor.

## FAZE/PHASE
- To faze is to disturb or unsettle.
- A phase is a stage; to phase (in/out) is to do gradually.

## FERMENT/FOMENT
- To ferment is to alcoholize (or, figuratively, agitate/seethe).
- To foment is to stir up (discord). Both verbs overlap in figurative use.

## FICTIONAL/FICTITIOUS
- "Fictional" describes works of imaginative art and their parts.
- "Fictitious" describes something made up outside art (a fake excuse).

## FLACK/FLAK
- A flack is a press agent.
- Flak is antiaircraft fire; harsh criticism is "flak."

## FLAIL/FLAY/FLOG
- To flail is to wave wildly (also to thresh or wallop).
- To flog is to whip; used with "flail" as a punishment synonym.
- To flay is to strip the skin off (literally or with words).

## FLAIR/FLARE
- Flair is a knack or stylishness.
- A flare is a burst of light/flame, a signal, or a widening.

## FLAUNT/FLOUT
- To flaunt is to show off.
- To flout is to defy or show contempt for (usually the law or rules).

## FLESH OUT/FLUSH OUT
- To flesh out is to add substance or detail.
- To flush out is to force out (of hiding, or by rinsing).

## FLIER/FLYER
- Use "flier" for something that flies, "flyer" for a handbill.
- For a risky gamble, "flyer" is slightly preferred.

## FLOUNDER/FOUNDER
- To flounder is to struggle clumsily.
- To founder is to sink or fail. (Floundering can precede foundering.)

## FORBEAR/FOREBEAR
- To forbear is to refrain or show restraint (noun: forbearance).
- One's forebears are one's ancestors.

## FOREGO/FORGO
- To forego is to precede.
- To forgo is to do without.

## FOREWORD/FORWARD
- A foreword is an introductory section of a book (usually by someone else).
- Forward is a direction (opposite of backward); as an adjective, presumptuous.

## GANTLET/GAUNTLET
- A gauntlet is a glove (thrown down or taken up in challenge).
- For running between two lines of attackers, either "gantlet" or "gauntlet" is used; "gauntlet" is more common.

## GEL/JELL
- A gel is a jelly (also a colored lighting sheet).
- To gel or jell is to take shape/set.

## GIBE/JIBE/JIVE
- A gibe is a taunt (verb: to deride).
- To jibe is to agree or align.
- "Jive" (talk/slang, music) is not a synonym for "jibe."

## GRAVELY/GRAVELLY
- "Gravely" (adverb) means seriously (gravely ill).
- "Gravelly" (adjective) means full of gravel, or rough (a gravelly voice).

## GRISLY/GRISTLY/GRIZZLY/GRIZZLED
- Grisly means gory.
- Gristly means full of gristle (tough meat).
- Grizzly is a kind of bear.
- Grizzled means gray-haired (not unkempt or rugged).

## HANGAR/HANGER
- A plane goes in a hangar.
- A coat goes on a hanger.

## HANGED/HUNG
- People are hanged (executed).
- Objects (paintings) are hung.

## HARDY/HEARTY
- Hardy means able to endure hardship; tough.
- Hearty means warm, spirited, robust (a hearty soup, a hearty welcome).

## HAWK/HOCK
- To hawk is to sell (or to peddle aggressively).
- To hock is to pawn. (A loogie can be hawked or hocked.)

## HISTORIC/HISTORICAL
- "Historic" means momentous (a historic event).
- "Historical" means existing in the past. Write "a historic," not "an historic."

## HOARD/HORDE
- To hoard is to amass; a hoard is the stash.
- A horde is a teeming crowd (usually derogatory).

## HOME/HONE
- To home in is to zero in on a target.
- To hone is to sharpen. The standard phrase is "home in on," not "hone in on."

## HUMMUS/HUMUS
- Hummus is a chickpea dip.
- Humus is decaying organic matter in soil.

## IMPLY/INFER
- To imply is to suggest without stating.
- To infer is to deduce from evidence. Speakers imply; listeners infer.

## INTERNMENT/INTERMENT
- Internment is confinement/imprisonment (esp. wartime).
- Interment is burial.

## IT'S/ITS
- "It's" means "it is."
- "Its" is the possessive of "it."

## KIBITZ/KIBBUTZ
- To kibitz (one b) is to chatter or offer unwanted advice.
- A kibbutz (two b's) is an Israeli collective farm.

## LAMA/LLAMA
- A llama is the South American animal.
- A lama is a Tibetan/Mongolian Buddhist monk.

## LAY, LIE, LAID, LAIN, AND THE REST OF THE CLAN
- "Lay" is transitive: it needs an object (you lay a thing down). If you can substitute "place," use "lay."
- "Lie" is intransitive: it needs no object (you lie down; you lie on the couch).
- Conjugations: lay/laid/laid; lie/lay/lain. (Note the trap: past tense of "lie" is "lay.")
- It's "lie low" and "lie in wait," but "lay a trap."

## LEACH/LEECH
- To leach is to drain a substance out via percolating liquid.
- To leech is to apply leeches, or (figuratively) to sponge off someone.

## LEAD/LED
- Past tense of "lead" is "led," not "lead" (they sound alike; the misspelling is common).

## LIGHTENING/LIGHTNING
- "Lightening" is making lighter (in weight or color).
- "Lightning" is the electrical flash in a storm.

## LOATH/LOATHE
- "Loath" (adjective) means reluctant.
- "Loathe" (verb) means to detest.

## LOSE/LOOSE
- To lose is to mislay.
- "Loose" (adjective) means not tight; to loose (verb) is to set free.

## LUXURIANT/LUXURIOUS
- Luxuriant means lush, abundant (luxuriant hair).
- Luxurious means lavish and expensive.

## MANTEL/MANTLE
- A mantel is the shelf above a fireplace.
- A mantle is a cloak, or (figuratively) a role/responsibility taken on.

## MARITAL/MARTIAL
- Marital relates to marriage.
- Martial relates to the military.

## MASTERFUL/MASTERLY
- Traditional split: "masterful" = domineering, "masterly" = skillful.
- In practice writers use "masterful" for skillful too; the distinction is weak. Either is defensible.

## MILITATE/MITIGATE
- To militate (against) is to work against or counteract.
- To mitigate is to alleviate. "Mitigate against" is always incorrect.

## MILLENNIUM/MILLENNIA
- "Millennium" is singular, "millennia" plural. Spelling: two l's, two n's.

## MINER/MINOR
- Miners work underground.
- Minors are children; "minor" also means small/inconsequential (or a musical key).

## MUCOUS/MUCUS
- "Mucous" is the adjective; "mucus" is the noun. Mucous membranes produce mucus.

## NAVAL/NAVEL
- "Naval" relates to the navy.
- A "navel" is a belly button.

## ONBOARD/ON BOARD
- "Onboard" is an adjective (onboard navigation).
- "On board" is an adverb (on board the ship; on board with the plan).

## ORDINANCE/ORDNANCE
- An ordinance is a decree or law.
- Ordnance is military supplies/artillery.

## PALATE/PALETTE/PALLET
- A palate is the roof of the mouth or sense of taste.
- A palette is an artist's color board or range of colors.
- A pallet is a loading platform (or an outmoded word for a small bed).

## PASS/PASSED/PAST
- "Passed" is the past tense of the verb "pass."
- "Past" is a noun, adjective, preposition, or adverb, never a verb.

## PEAK/PEEK/PIQUE
- A peak is a summit.
- A peek is a glance (it's "sneak peek," not "sneak peak").
- Pique is a fit of irritation; to pique is to stimulate (pique one's interest).

## PEAL/PEEL
- Bells peal.
- Potatoes are peeled; the skin removed is a peel. Watchful eyes are "peeled."

## PEDAL, PEDDLE, PEDDLER, ET AL.
- A pedal is worked by foot; to pedal is to work one.
- To peddle is to sell goods around, or to push a dubious idea. Sellers are peddlers.
- To backpedal is to retreat from a position; to soft-pedal is to downplay.
- A petal is a flower segment.

## PHENOMENON/PHENOMENA
- "Phenomenon" is singular; "phenomena" is plural.

## PIXILATED/PIXELATED
- "Pixilated" means confusedly, whimsically crazy.
- "Pixelated" describes a digital image broken into visible blocks.

## PLUM/PLUMB/PLUMMY
- "Plum" (adjective) means choice, desirable (a plum job).
- To plumb is to measure depth or explore deeply; "plumb" as adverb means utterly/squarely, as adjective means exactly vertical. A plumber plumbs.
- A "plummy" voice is affectedly rich and proper.

## POKEY/POKY
- The "pokey" is prison.
- "Poky" means annoyingly slow, or drab.

## POPULACE/POPULOUS
- "Populace" (noun) means the population or common people.
- "Populous" (adjective) means densely populated.

## PORE/POUR
- To pore over something is to study it closely (pores are also skin openings).
- To pour is to tip liquid out of a container.

## PRECEDE/PROCEED
- To precede is to come before.
- To proceed is to move forward.

## PREMIER/PREMIERE
- "Premier" (adjective) means top-ranked; (noun) a head of state.
- A "premiere" is a debut.

## PRESCRIBE/PROSCRIBE
- To prescribe is to direct authoritatively (medicine, treatment).
- To proscribe is to forbid.

## PRINCIPAL/PRINCIPLE
- "Principal" means primary/chief (a person, a sum of money, a lead role).
- A "principle" is a fundamental truth or moral rule.

## PRONE/SUPINE
- Supine is lying on one's back.
- Prone is lying face-down. (Very commonly confused; check a dictionary if unsure.)

## PROPHECY/PROPHESY
- "Prophecy" is the noun.
- "Prophesy" is the verb. (An oracle prophesies a prophecy.)

## RACK/WRACK/WREAK
- "Rack" relates to torture/strain: racked with pain, rack one's brains, nerve-racking.
- To wrack is to wreck/destroy ("wrack and ruin"); dictionaries now often treat rack/wrack as synonyms. When you mean wreck, use "wreck."
- To wreak is to inflict (wreak havoc); past tense is "wreaked," not "wrought."

## REIGN/REIN
- Monarchs reign.
- Horses are reined; the phrase is "free rein" (not "free reign").

## RELUCTANT/RETICENT
- Reluctant means unwilling.
- Reticent means uncommunicative, reserved. One is reluctant to do X, reticent about Y.

## RETCH/WRETCH
- To retch is to gag or nearly vomit.
- A wretch is a miserable or contemptible person.

## RIFFLE/RIFLE
- To riffle is to flip lightly through (pages, cards).
- To rifle is to rummage in order to steal.

## ROGUE/ROUGE
- A rogue is a scoundrel.
- Rouge is red cheek/lip makeup.

## SEGUE/SEGWAY
- A "segue" is a smooth transition (verb or noun).
- "Segway" is the scooter, never a synonym for segue.

## SENSUAL/SENSUOUS
- "Sensual" pertains to physical (often sexual) gratification.
- "Sensuous" pertains to aesthetic pleasure of the senses. (Widely confused; consider another word if clarity matters.)

## SHONE/SHOWN
- "Shone" (or "shined") is the past of "shine."
- "Shown" is the past participle of "show."

## STANCH/STAUNCH
- To stanch is to stop a flow (blood) or hold in check.
- "Staunch" (adjective) means steadfast, loyal.

## STATIONARY/STATIONERY
- "Stationary" means unmoving.
- "Stationery" is writing paper.

## SUBTLY/SUBTLETY
- "Subtly" is the adverb.
- "Subtlety" is the noun. (Mostly a typing pitfall.)

## TENANT/TENET
- A tenant rents.
- A tenet is a belief or principle.

## THAN/THEN
- "Than" is for comparisons (the correct construction is "no sooner...than").
- "Then" is about time or sequence.

## THEIR/THERE/THEY'RE
- "Their" is possessive (their house).
- "There" indicates place (over there).
- "They're" means "they are."

## TO/TOO
- "To" is the preposition/infinitive marker (walk to the store; to be).
- "Too" means also or excessively.

## TOOTHY/TOOTHSOME
- Toothy means having prominent or many teeth.
- Toothsome means tasty (or, by extension, attractive).

## TORTUOUS/TORTUROUS
- "Tortuous" means twisting, winding (neutral).
- "Torturous" means like torture (unpleasant).

## UNDERWAY/UNDER WAY
- "Underway" is an adjective; "under way" is an adverb. You usually want "under way" (the project is under way).

## VALE/VEIL
- A vale is a valley.
- A veil is a face covering. The phrase is "vale of tears," not "veil of tears."

## VENAL/VENIAL
- "Venal" means corrupt, bribable.
- "Venial" means pardonable (a venial sin).

## WAIVE/WAVE/WAVER
- To waive is to give up (a right).
- To wave is to move one's hand (an inspector waves you through, not waives).
- To waver is to tremble or vacillate (a waiver is a document).

## WHOSE/WHO'S
- "Whose" is the possessive.
- "Who's" means "who is."

## WORKOUT/WORK OUT
- "Workout" is the noun.
- "Work out" is the verb. You work out to get a workout.

## YOUR/YOU'RE
- "Your" is possessive.
- "You're" means "you are." (Same pattern as whose/who's.)
